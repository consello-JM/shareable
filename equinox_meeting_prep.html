2. Our Process

Three phases

PhaseLengthCoversPrice
Discover~6 weeksWS1 + WS2$250K
Design~8 weeksWS3 + WS4$400K
Roadmap~3 weeksWS5$200K
Sum of individual phases$850K
Bundled Total, all three together$750K

$750K is the bundled price when all three phases are taken together. Taking any two of the three carries a 7.5% discount off those two phases' combined price. Running a pilot and full implementation are priced separately as additional scope beyond the three phases.

Five decision gates

  • Gate 1 (week ~3): Build-upon vs. complete redesign, the highest-leverage call in the engagement.
  • Gate 2 (week ~6): Is the third-party segmentation data sufficient to proceed on.
  • Gate 3 (weeks 8–10): Continue as scoped, or redirect, based on early hypotheses.
  • Gate 4 (week ~14): Architecture approved; pilot or straight to full implementation.
  • Gate 5 (post-pilot): Go/no-go on full implementation, fee contingent on this call.
The throughline: we're recommending one clear plan, with acceleration available as a real, named tradeoff: less complete Discover in exchange for speed.

The tier-naming problem, concrete examples

  • "All Access" does not mean all access.
  • "Destination" does not mean every destination; there's a separate "Destination-plus-Yards" tier layered on top.
  • Newer amenities, contrast therapy, IV drips, red-light recovery, tend to land in whichever tier is newest, unrelated to what members would actually value.

Assets that exist but aren't tied together

Personal training, Pilates, spa, retail, Optimize, and the Circle partnership roster (BILT, Flex) each perform and generate revenue independently. None is integrated into a single membership ladder with an upgrade path.

5. Best Practices, Other Industries

Four examples, each chosen to prove a different piece of the argument, and each with a direct, specific implication for Equinox, not just a general lesson.

Amazon Prime

Proves: bundling breadth itself is the moat.

$49.6B

FY2025 subscription revenue, up ~12% YoY. 240M+ paying members. Over 80% of members use 2+ benefits; the more perks a member uses, the more they spend overall.

Equinox takeaway: the bundling ingredients already exist, PT, Pilates, spa, retail, Optimize, Circle. The fix is packaging what's already there into one visible bundle.

EMARKETER/Bizrate, May 2026; Amazon IR, Q4 2025; BusinessStats, May 2026

Costco

Proves: mix-shift to a higher tier is self-funding and compounds faster than base growth.

74.2%

Share of worldwide sales from Executive members, who are only 47.7% of the paid base and growing more than 2x faster (9.6% vs. 4.1% YoY) than total membership. US renewal rate 92%+.

Equinox takeaway: this is direct proof the recommended fix, mix-shift over another rate increase, works at real scale, already, not as an untested theory.

Costco Q3 FY2026 results, longyield.substack.com, May 2026; Yahoo Finance, Oct 2025

Marriott Bonvoy

Proves: a real, current model for protecting members through a tier change without losing them outright.

2026 "soft landing" policy: members who don't requalify for elite status drop only one tier, never lose status entirely in one step.

Equinox takeaway: this is the direct model for how Equinox migrates existing members through whatever the new architecture turns out to be, whichever way Gate 1 lands.

LoyaltyLobby / One Mile at a Time, Nov 2025

Other considerations

Dimensions worth checking against, beyond the four examples here:

  • Sizes
  • Numbers (of tiers)
  • Naming conventions
  • Benefits and types of benefits
  • Qualification
  • Mobility
  • Clarity in offering

Delta SkyMiles Medallion

Proves: naming clarity and disproportionate escalation, both, in one structure.

Four tiers, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Diamond, at 5,000 / 10,000 / 15,000 / 28,000 MQDs. Each step up unlocks disproportionately more value, Diamond's benefits (Sky Club access, upgrade certificates) are worth far more than 2.8x Silver's, despite the MQD requirement only being 2.8x higher.

Equinox takeaway: a well-tested tier count (four), instantly legible names with no explanation needed, and value that escalates faster than the price does, a direct contrast to Select/Regional/All Access/Destination, names that signal hierarchy far less clearly and, per the tier-naming problem already flagged, deliver something different from what they promise.

Delta Air Lines Medallion Program Overview, 2026; The Points Guy, Jan 2026
On tier count and clarity specifically: four is a well-tested number, enough to create real aspiration without decision paralysis. Delta publishes its full tier-by-benefit comparison openly; Equinox has no public rate card or amenity census (see Lizzie's deck, Sources & Confidence). That gap in public clarity is itself part of the tier-naming problem this proposal already surfaces.

6. Our POV: What to Cut First If Asked to Expedite

The pilot is the cleanest first cut, it's a separable, later-stage decision that doesn't touch the evidence base at all. Discover's benchmarking depth is the last lever to reach for, not the first: it's a named RFP deliverable (WS1), and it's the evidence base the whole Section 1 argument rests on, the Life Time mechanism, the Costco proof, the Soho House model. Thinning it doesn't just save time, it weakens the credibility of the recommendation itself.

  1. The pilot itself. Skipping it (Gate 4) is faster and cheaper, but removes the live-market proof Silver Lake likely wants. If speed is the priority and investor optics aren't, this is the first lever, contained on its own, the evidence upstream stays untouched.
  2. Design's own pace, via overlap with Discover. Real compression, real rework risk if later findings shift direction. This is where the accelerated case's premium comes from, a real cost, contained to Design's own execution, separate from the evidence underneath it.
  3. Depth of Discover's benchmarking. Compressible via raw segmentation data instead of the September deliverable, but this is the last lever to reach for. It's the foundation the recommendation stands on, and it's explicitly named in the RFP.
  4. What we would not recommend cutting: Roadmap. It's already the shortest phase (~3 weeks) and carries the actual execution planning Equinox specifically flagged as the weak muscle. Compressing this further risks reproducing the exact gap this engagement exists to close.

7. Gaps Between What We Presented and the RFP's Full Ask

Checked against the RFP's actual workstream list. These are the places we're thinner than the ask.

  • WS5 asks for sales model and capability implications specifically. We have field enablement covered; the sales model itself, how selling actually changes once the tiers change, still needs its own piece of work.
  • WS5 also asks for Experience KPIs, named separately from revenue and retention KPIs. Our current KPI framework is a single combined dashboard; we haven't broken out an experience-specific category.
  • WS4 asks for scalability as one of the evaluation lenses alongside member value, brand fit, economics, and feasibility. We've covered the other four explicitly; scalability still needs to be named as its own filter.
  • The RFP's Engagement Model names Eswar Veluri and Bindu Shah as project sponsors. Neither is acknowledged by name anywhere in the proposal itself.

One question about our own logic, worth being ready to defend directly:

  • What happens if the segmentation data isn't good enough when it lands? Gate 2 exists for exactly this: supplemental research gets scoped and priced as an addition.
