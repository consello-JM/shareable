Subway (US)

Growth Opportunity Analysis — Brand, Experience & Growth

Prepared from public sources Internal only — strip confidence scores before anything external September 3, 2026

Subway's new leadership ran the exact play the earlier thesis called for, a modernized Sub Club loyalty program as a frequency engine, and it broke inside four months. Not because customers rejected it. Because the mechanic was too generous for franchisee margins, franchisees revolted, and corporate walked it back twice. That's not a reason to drop the loyalty thesis. It's dated proof of the exact risk the original framing flagged, and a mandate to fix the program's economics rather than pitch a new one from scratch.

Five opportunities below have real 2025–2026 evidence behind them, not ten. Two more are directionally right but under-evidenced and sit on a watch list, not in a pitch.

The Five Opportunities

Each write-up follows the standard: what it is, why now, impact by audience, deliverables, comps, competitive framing, and risks stated without hedging.

What it is

Redesign the Sub Club mechanic so it drives frequency without breaking franchisee unit economics, and build the redesign process around visible franchisee input this time.

Why now

Subway's own president, Damien Harmon, told franchisees in February 2026 the program was moving to "an interim solution" while the company works with its advisory council on "the next evolution of Sub Club." Corporate has already admitted the current version doesn't work. The mandate is open.

Impact

INTERNAL

The program brought in more than 1 million new loyalty members in its first weeks — proof the frequency mechanic works on the demand side. The job is keeping that acquisition rate without the redemption cost that triggered the revolt.

EXTERNAL

Public sentiment on the walk-back was sharply negative; some customers said they'd switch to Jersey Mike's. A stable redesign avoids a third public reversal.

INVESTOR

A franchisee-financed loyalty program reversed twice in four months signals weak internal decision discipline to an owner two years into a turnaround.

What the work could look like

  • Unit economics model for loyalty mechanics (redemption cost per member vs. incremental visit lift) built on real AUV and margin data, not assumptions
  • A franchisee-tested pilot process before any national relaunch, given the trust deficit
  • A points/reward structure benchmarked against what actually works elsewhere in QSR loyalty, not just the pre-2005 Subway stamp card

Comps & competitive framing

Firehouse Subs (RBI) is explicitly building toward first-party-data-driven targeted offers in 2026 rather than blanket discounts. Jersey Mike's core customer visits more than 6 times a year on a $200M national ad fund and celebrity ambassador strategy, not a loyalty-discount strategy — the chain gaining the most share isn't winning on giveaways.

Risks

  • Consello has no visibility into Subway's actual redemption economics; a specific mechanic proposal without that data is a guess dressed as a recommendation.
  • Franchisee trust is currently adversarial. A redesign not co-built with NAASF or the Franchise Advisory Council repeats the exact failure that just happened.
  • A third public change to the same program in under a year carries its own reputational cost, independent of whether the new mechanic is better.

What it is

Build the demand-side case, traffic, AUV recovery, loyalty-driven frequency, that makes remodel capital an easy yes for franchisees in the stores Subway is choosing to keep, rather than treating rightsizing as a real estate decision alone.

Why now

Subway closed a net 729 US locations in the year covered by its April 2026 franchise disclosure document, the tenth straight year of decline, taking the system to 18,773 restaurants, down from a 2015 peak above 27,000. Corporate frames the closures as keeping only restaurants with the real estate, visibility, and operations to succeed, not just shrinking. That means the survivors are the investment thesis now.

Impact

INTERNAL

Fewer, better-located stores only pays off if AUV per remaining store climbs. Jersey Mike's generates at least $300,000 more revenue per unit annually than Subway or Firehouse — that gap is the number a remodel case needs to close.

EXTERNAL

Subway is still the largest US restaurant chain by unit count, ahead of Starbucks and McDonald's. That's a marketing asset if the surviving footprint performs, a liability story if it doesn't.

INVESTOR

A "rightsizing" narrative reads very differently to Roark depending on whether AUV actually recovers. That proof doesn't exist publicly yet.

Deliverables

  • AUV recovery model by remaining-store cohort
  • Remodel ROI case tied to demonstrated traffic lift, not corporate mandate
  • Franchisee-facing investment narrative built for a system with active trust problems

Comps

Jersey Mike's runs 3,227 US units on AUV of $1.37M (2025), up 3.2% year over year — the opposite growth pattern (fewer, higher-volume stores) from Subway's historical playbook.

Risks

  • Closures reduce near-term system sales and franchisee count, a hard story regardless of long-run AUV logic.
  • Asking franchisees for remodel capital right when trust in corporate is at a low point is a sequencing problem, not just a financing one.

What it is

A structured process for rebuilding the corporate-to-franchisee relationship, distinct from any single program fix.

Why now

In January 2026, franchisees representing more than 5,000 restaurants, over a quarter of the US system, signed a petition through NAASF asking corporate to change the Sub Club terms and to re-engage, stating corporate hadn't spoken with the group "in years." NAASF's chairman wrote directly to leadership the same month.

Open question worth flagging directly Harmon's February memo references working with "the franchise advisory council," a different, apparently internal body from NAASF. Public reporting doesn't make clear whether Subway is engaging the group that's actually angry, or a separate internal council instead. That distinction matters and Consello shouldn't propose a fix without knowing which forum has real standing.

Impact

INTERNAL

Every program change touching franchisee economics, loyalty, value menu, remodel capital, runs through this relationship. Fixing the forum is a multiplier on everything else on this list.

EXTERNAL

A public franchisee petition covering over a quarter of the system is visible in press coverage regardless of what corporate says internally.

INVESTOR

This kind of signal shows up in FDD litigation disclosures. Owners notice.

Note on ownership

Arguably a better fit for Consello's Talent & Executive Search or CEO Advisory practice than BEG, since it's a governance and relationship-design problem more than a growth or brand problem. Flagging it here because ignoring it makes the other four recommendations naive, not because BEG should own execution.

Risks

  • Recommending "re-engage franchisees" without confirming which body has standing risks proposing a fix to the wrong audience.
  • Trust repair after a "no contact in years" complaint is a multi-quarter process. A single town hall or campaign won't fix it and shouldn't be pitched as if it will.

What it is

Support the marketing reorganization already underway rather than propose a parallel one.

Why now

Global CMO Greg Lyons left in May 2026, about a year into the role. CEO Jonathan Fitzpatrick eliminated the global CMO position in favor of a regional structure. Jeff Klein (former Popeyes president) came in as US CMO; David Skena, previously North America CMO, moved up to Chief Strategy and Chief Commercial Officer. Omnicom now holds US media and CRM, and a creative review is underway, reportedly aimed at rebuilding cultural and emotional relevance, especially among younger consumers.

Impact

INTERNAL

A second major agency and leadership change inside 18 months only pays off if it produces a coherent brand voice fast; consecutive reorganizations without visible output read as instability to already-skeptical franchisees.

EXTERNAL

The stated goal, relevance with younger consumers, is measurable. Track it, don't just assert it.

INVESTOR

Marketing spend efficiency under a consolidated agency model is a real line item Roark will ask about.

Deliverables

  • A marketing accountability framework tying the regional CMO structure and Omnicom relationship to specific traffic and brand-health metrics
  • An incentive-alignment review between the new CRM agency relationship and the Sub Club redesign work in Opportunity 1, since CRM and loyalty now sit under the same agency

Risks

  • Two leadership changes in the CMO seat within a year raises the real cost of a third disruption; any recommendation needs to work with the current structure, not replace it again.
  • No public data yet shows the Omnicom move or creative review has moved a business metric. This is a bet on a process, not a proven outcome, and should be scored that way internally even though the org signal itself is well evidenced.

What it is

Build the case for how Subway's new Value Menu and "Meal of the Day" promotion (launched April 2026) should be priced and sequenced so they lift traffic without repeating the Sub Club margin problem.

Why now

An independent restaurant-research note (NoBullEconomics, May 2026) frames Subway's core problem as an unsolved value equation: convince customers it's as good as Jersey Mike's, or price below it. The current answer, a Value Menu plus a health/protein-forward update, is unproven. The same note calls it a step in the right direction but says results must be proven.

Push back on the framing, worth saying directly The share data argues against the low-price lane. Jersey Mike's is winning share at a premium, not a discount, roughly a high-teens to mid-20s percentage premium to Subway in at least one regional pricing comparison. If the category leader gaining share is proving people will pay more for a better product, a value-menu-led response may be solving the wrong problem.

Impact

INTERNAL

A value menu compresses margin per transaction. If it doesn't lift traffic enough to offset, franchisee profitability, the exact issue that broke Sub Club, gets worse, not better.

EXTERNAL

Subway still holds roughly 53% share of the domestic sub-sandwich segment's system-wide sales by one analyst estimate (unverified against a second source). Even a modest share defense is worth a lot given that base.

INVESTOR

Whether Subway competes on price or product credibility is a real strategic fork Roark should see argued explicitly, not assumed.

Deliverables

  • Price-elasticity read on the new Value Menu against Jersey Mike's premium positioning
  • Franchisee margin model showing the traffic lift required to offset new discount depth
  • A recommendation on whether Subway should compete on price or on product credibility, not both at once

Risks

  • The strategic rationale here comes from one secondary analyst source, not a Subway executive statement. Treat the "why" as inference until corroborated.
  • A value-led response risks reinforcing exactly the cheap, low-quality perception Subway is also trying to shed with its protein/health menu positioning. The two moves can work against each other if not sequenced deliberately.

Horizon Ideas & Exclusions

Directionally right, but not ready to pitch, or cut outright rather than padded in.

First-party data / retail media 76% — horizon

Firehouse Subs' CEO said at a 2026 investor day the chain will lean harder into first-party data and targeted offers this year, calling it a possible "big tailwind." That's real, but it's Firehouse's stated plan, not Subway's. No Subway executive has signaled this as a near-term priority in anything found. Worth watching, not worth pitching yet. If the Sub Club redesign (Opportunity 1) produces a clean loyalty data asset, this becomes a natural next opportunity, not before.

Digital ordering / app platform excluded — insufficient sourcing

No tier-1, dated (last 12 months) reporting found on Subway's actual digital sales mix, app engagement, or platform investment plans. Excluding rather than guessing at a number. Internal Subway data would change this.

International / master franchisee expansion out of scope

Real, backed by the historical 4,000-unit China deal and Roark's stated intent to support international growth, but outside a US-focused BEG engagement and requires cross-border operational staffing Consello can't deploy quickly.

Corporate org design beyond marketing out of scope

The marketing reorg is well evidenced; a broader HQ restructuring recommendation isn't, and shouldn't be invented to round the list out to ten.

What Changed Since the Original Thesis

The prior framing: Sub Club as a frequency engine, the loyalty file as a first-party data asset, marketing as an accountable operating model.

Sub Club got tested, and broke on the franchisee side, not the customer side

Corporate relaunched Sub Club in December 2025: buy 3 footlongs (or 6 six-inch), get a 4th free, stacked with a separate points system. Franchisees called it financial suicide on the internal comment board within weeks. Customers loved it and got angry when it was cut. The failure mode was P&L design, not consumer appetite. That's a solvable problem, not a dead thesis.

Marketing as an accountable operating model is now literally happening

Just not confirmed to involve Consello. Global CMO role eliminated, agency of record moved to Omnicom for US media and CRM, a creative review underway. If it isn't already known whether Consello has a seat in that process, that's the first call to make, not a slide.

The loyalty-file thesis holds; the constraint isn't data, it's trust

Franchisees representing 5,000+ locations petitioned corporate in January 2026, saying corporate hadn't engaged NAASF "in years." Any data or loyalty recommendation that doesn't address that relationship gets rejected before it gets read.
